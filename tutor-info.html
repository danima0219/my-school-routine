Tutor Information

Project rationale and evidence-informed design specifications

Functional Specifications

This table compares the evidence-informed functional specifications with the current implementation of My School Routine. It identifies existing features, implementation limitations and recommended improvements. Supporting sources provide the rationale for design decisions, but do not necessarily validate the effectiveness of the prototype.

Specification Supporting Sources Functional Requirement Current Prototype Recommended Improvements
E-01: Linear and Uncluttered Navigation Dzaman et al. (2022); CAST (2024). These sources provide a basis for organising accessible learning experiences and anticipating barriers. Provide a predictable activity sequence that minimises unnecessary navigation and keeps the learner focused on the current routine and learning objective. The routine sequence is presented on one screen. External information and educator settings are also accessible, introducing navigation options unrelated to the immediate activity. Develop a learner-focused view that separates the activity from educator information and configuration options. Preserve educator access without unnecessarily interrupting the activity.
E-02: Explicit Next-Step Assessment Trigger Dzaman et al. (2022), concerning assessment and UDL; CAST (2024), Guidelines 5.1 and 6.4. Support accessible response options and monitoring progress, but do not independently establish the effectiveness of a mandatory next-step question for this specific population. Present an explicit question asking the learner to identify the next step in a familiar school routine. Record the response before marking the assessment opportunity as completed, while allowing appropriate communication and support options. The "What comes next?" question requires a response before it can be recorded. Only one question is currently available. Extend the activity to include additional routine steps and assessment opportunities. Ensure that the response mechanism accommodates the learner's available communication methods. Do not automatically interpret an absent response as a lack of understanding.
E-03: Dual-Factor Performance Logging CAST (2024), Guidelines 5.3 and 6.4; Dzaman et al. (2022), concerning assessment and UDL. These sources support graduated assistance and monitoring progress. The specific support categories are project-defined and require operational definitions. Record response accuracy and level of support as separate dimensions. Use clearly defined categories: Independent, Verbal Prompt, Gestural Support and Physical Assistance. Document performance in relation to the intended learning outcome. The log records accuracy and support level, distinguishing independent correct responses from supported correct responses. Define each support category and establish consistent recording procedures. Include procedures for incorrect responses, no response and responses that cannot be assessed. Interpret performance in relation to the learning outcome rather than interface navigation alone.
E-04: Adaptable Visual Representation Options CAST (2024), Guidelines 2.5 and 4.2; Knight et al. (2015). Knight et al. provides evidence concerning visual activity schedules for autistic individuals. Allow educators to select and change photographs, AAC symbols or other appropriate visual representations for individual routine steps, according to the learner's communication needs, familiarity and preferences. Representation options are present, but the photograph option is a placeholder and switching between representations is not fully functional. Implement reliable visual switching and allow educators to select appropriate representations for individual learners. Verify that each option displays correctly and remains consistent throughout the activity. Avoid assuming that one representation type is universally preferable.
E-05: Consistent Visual Card Layout and Readability Dzaman et al. (2022), concerning typography, colour and contrast; CAST (2024), Guideline 2.1. These sources provide general principles for accessible presentation and clarifying information. Maintain consistent card dimensions, spacing, borders and typography. Ensure that text and visual representations remain legible across intended devices and that essential information remains available when text is resized. The prototype uses consistent card layouts, 4:3 aspect ratios, padding and borders. Text sizes do not consistently meet the proposed 24 pt minimum. Review typography and card dimensions against the project's specification. Treat 24 pt as a project-specific design target requiring justification and testing, rather than a universal accessibility requirement. Test readability, scaling and layout consistency on intended devices.
E-06: Purposeful Multimedia and Audio Controls Dzaman et al. (2022), concerning multimedia in technology-enabled learning; CAST (2024), Guidelines 2.5 and 7.4. These sources support purposeful use of multimedia and consideration of distractions, rather than universal exclusion of audio or animation. Avoid unnecessary decorative multimedia. Include audio, animation or other media when they serve an instructional or accessibility purpose. Provide appropriate control over their use. The prototype does not use background music or decorative animation. Selecting a response automatically triggers browser speech synthesis. Evaluate whether automatic speech output is appropriate for different learners. Consider educator controls for enabling, disabling or manually activating speech. Verify that audio supports the intended activity without unnecessarily interrupting engagement or communication.
E-07: Accessible Response Methods and Assistive Technology Compatibility CAST (2024), Guidelines 4.1, 4.2 and 5.1; Pope et al. (2025) examines interventions combining naturalistic developmental behavioural approaches and aided AAC. Therrien et al.(2025) reviews AAC interventions using speech-generating devices with autistic preschoolers. Support appropriate response methods, including touch, pointer and keyboard interaction, and investigate compatibility with switch access and relevant AAC technologies. Allow participation through the learner's available communication methods without requiring a single response modality. Standard HTML buttons support common pointer and keyboard interactions. Dedicated switch access and direct AAC integration have not been implemented or verified. Test keyboard navigation and relevant assistive technologies. Investigate switch-access compatibility and the feasibility of AAC integration. Document which methods have been tested and avoid claiming compatibility until it has been verified.
E-08: Technical Accessibility and WCAG 2.2 Alignment (World Wide Web Consortium [W3C], 2024); CAST (2024). WCAG provides the technical benchmark for applicable accessibility criteria, while CAST contributes the broader inclusive-learning rationale. Provide accessible interaction targets, sufficient text and interface contrast, visible keyboard focus, appropriate accessible names and keyboard-operable functionality. Evaluate the applicable WCAG 2.2 success criteria. Buttons and select elements have a minimum height of 48 CSS pixels, visible focus styling and reduced-motion support. Contrast and full WCAG 2.2 conformance have not been verified. Conduct a structured accessibility audit. Test contrast ratios, target dimensions and spacing, keyboard operation, focus visibility, accessible names and relevant assistive-technology compatibility. Document the criteria tested and outstanding issues before making a conformance claim.
E-09: Interpretable Performance Reporting CAST (2024), Guideline 6.4; Dzaman et al. (2022), concerning assessment and UDL. These sources support monitoring progress and accessible assessment. The reporting fields and CSV structure are project-specific design decisions. Ensure that exported reports distinguish response accuracy from the level of support provided. Enable educators to identify independent correct responses, supported correct responses, incorrect responses and responses that were not assessed, where applicable. The on-screen log identifies independent and supported correct responses. CSV fields allow these categories to be interpreted together, but an explicit exported performance category is not currently provided. Add an explicit performance category to the exported report while retaining separate fields for accuracy and support level. Document how each category should be interpreted. Ensure that missing responses are not automatically classified as incorrect or as evidence of insufficient understanding.

Evaluation Note

The implementation status reflects inspection of the current prototype code. Accessibility, usability and learning effectiveness require separate testing. The cited literature informs design decisions but does not establish that the prototype has been validated for autistic children aged 3 to 5 who use AAC.
